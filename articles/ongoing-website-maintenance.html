Website support

What ongoing website maintenance should include

A website needs content, operational, and technical ownership after launch—not just occasional software updates.

Maintenance should protect the website's ability to represent the business, serve visitors, generate opportunities, and connect to the systems behind it. The appropriate cadence depends on how often the business and the website change.

Content accuracy

Services, team information, locations, offers, pricing language, proof, policies, and calls to action become inaccurate over time. Give someone responsibility for reviewing important pages and publishing changes before outdated information creates confusion.

Landing pages and business changes

New campaigns, services, audiences, and sales initiatives should not be forced into the original launch structure. Ongoing support can turn those changes into focused pages and reusable content patterns without eroding the design system.

Security and dependencies

Platforms, libraries, plugins, hosting environments, domains, certificates, and credentials all have maintenance needs. Track ownership, apply relevant updates, remove unused access, back up what matters, and verify recovery rather than assuming it exists.

Forms and integrations

Test important forms, scheduling links, CRM delivery, email notifications, payments, and automation paths. A page can look healthy while qualified inquiries quietly stop reaching the business. Monitor failures and keep a clear fallback.

Performance and availability

New media, scripts, tags, and integrations can gradually slow a website down. Review asset weight, loading behavior, errors, and uptime after changes. Performance work should prioritize the pages and interactions that matter to visitors.

Accessibility

New content and components can introduce keyboard, focus, labeling, contrast, heading, and media issues even when the original site was reviewed. Include accessibility checks in the publishing and component process rather than treating them as a one-time launch activity.

Technical SEO

Watch for broken links, accidental indexing changes, incorrect canonicals, missing metadata, redirect issues, sitemap gaps, and duplicated or outdated pages. Technical checks cannot create demand by themselves, but they can prevent the site from undermining good content.

Analytics and conversion review

Use analytics to ask practical questions: which pages attract qualified visitors, which calls to action they choose, where forms fail, and which content supports real conversations. Respect consent choices and avoid sending sensitive form details into analytics tools.

Define the support agreement

Clarify what is monitored, how requests enter the queue, expected response windows, who approves changes, which third-party accounts the client owns, and what counts as a new project. A small, explicit agreement is more useful than vague “ongoing support.”

Maintenance should include improvement

Protecting the current site is only half the job. Use customer questions, sales feedback, search behavior, and conversion evidence to improve content and journeys over time. A maintained website stays current; an owned website becomes more useful.
